5. Gross Margin vs. Operating Margin: Core Principles & Distinctions

Gross margin measures profit after deducting only the direct production costs, while operating margin measures profit after deducting both direct costs and regular operating expenses.

Metric Gross Margin Operating Margin
Formula ((Revenue - COGS) / Revenue) × 100% (Operating Income (EBIT) / Revenue) × 100%
Costs Deducted Only COGS (raw materials, direct factory labor, direct manufacturing overhead) COGS + Operating Expenses (OPEX) (SG&A, R&D, marketing, sales commissions, depreciation)
Costs Excluded OPEX, non-operating expenses, interest, taxes Non-operating expenses, interest, taxes
What It Measures Production & pricing efficiency: how cheaply a company produces a good or service relative to selling price Overall business operational efficiency: how well management runs daily core operations
Typical Level Higher (typically top of the income statement) Lower (sits below gross profit on the income statement)

• Gross Margin in practice: If a company makes $100 in revenue and spends $40 on direct manufacturing costs (COGS), its gross profit is $60, giving a 60% gross margin.

• Operating Margin in practice: If that same company also spends $30 on administrative salaries, office rent, and marketing (OPEX), its operating profit (EBIT) is $30, giving a 30% operating margin.

Neither metric includes debt interest or corporate taxes—those are deducted further down the income statement to arrive at Net Profit Margin.

2. Scale Sanitization & Financial Sanity Checks
Auto Guardrails

Automatically detects reporting units in table headers to eliminate 1,000× or 1,000,000× scale distortion:

  • In Thousands (千元): Multiplies by $10^{-3}$ to normalize into $M (e.g. Palantir).
  • In Millions (百萬元): Direct 1.0 multiplier (Standard SEC & Taiwan TWSE).
  • In Billions / Trillions (十億/兆元): Multiplies by $10^3$ (e.g. Foxconn NT$6.86T).
  • Sanity Guardrail: Verifies $GP \le Revenue$ and $GM\% \in [0\%, 100\%]$ to safeguard $/FTE metrics.

7. Why 20-F / 10-K / 10-Q Parsing Still Needs LLM Semantic Dynamic Correction

Dual-Track Engine

While our parser structurally converts 20-F, 10-K, and 10-Q tables into flawless Markdown, real-world corporate SEC filings exhibit non-standard line items and unstructured narratives that require LLM (Gemini / Claude / GPT) semantic deduction:

Challenge Scenario Rule / Regex Bottleneck LLM Semantic Dynamic Solution
1. Non-Standard Accounting Items Google & Meta omit the Gross Profit line, reporting only Revenues and Cost of revenues. Pure regex returns 0% GM. Deduces accounting relations automatically: Gross Profit = Revenues - Cost of revenues.
2. Unstructured Headcount Text NVIDIA, AMD bury workforce totals in natural language prose across Item 1 / Item 6 without standard table grids. Understands sentence semantics to isolate total employees, R&D staff, and factory personnel accurately.
3. Dynamic Segment Sales Breakdown Each company has completely unique business segments (Google: Search/Cloud; Amazon: AWS/Retail; ASML: EUV/DUV). Parses Segment Notes to disaggregate multi-tier product revenue and map to sales_breakdown.
4. Unit Scale & Currency Traps Filings mix in thousands (e.g. Palantir), in millions, or foreign currencies (JPY, TWD, EUR). Performs financial sanity checks against table headers to prevent 1,000× scale distortion in Productivity ($/FTE).
5. OpEx Strategy & Lean Maturity Qualitative management commentary in Item 7 (MD&A) cannot be parsed by numerical regular expressions. Evaluates qualitative manufacturing reforms, automation progress, and generates 5-Stage Lean Maturity ratings.

• The Dual-Track Synergy: PDF-to-Markdown Parser handles structural table fidelity; LLM acts as the senior financial analyst brain to dynamically infer non-standard metrics, ensuring 100% universal adaptability for any global enterprise.
